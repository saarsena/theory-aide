Counterpoint: making two melodies work together

Counterpoint is the craft of writing two or more melodies that sound good at the same time and each sound like their own melody. It's not about following old rules for their own sake — it's about independence. When two lines stop being independent, your ear hears one thick line instead of two, and the music loses a voice.

The one rule that matters most

Most counterpoint guidelines boil down to one idea: don't let two voices accidentally fuse. The fastest way to fuse them is parallel fifths and parallel octaves: when two voices move in the same direction while staying a fifth or an octave apart, those intervals are so acoustically "empty" that the voices lock together. The opposite — contrary motion — is what keeps lines feeling separate and alive.
