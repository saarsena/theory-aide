Four personalities from one added note. The major 7th shimmers, the
flat 7th on a major triad churns, the flat 7th on a minor triad relaxes,
and the half-diminished broods. A [[seventh chord]] is nothing more
than a triad plus one more skip up the scale, and everything in this
article follows from which triad you started with and which seventh
landed on it.

## Four flavors, free with the key

Just as [[roman-numerals|Roman numerals]] showed the key handing you
seven triads for free, the key hands you seven sevenths, one per degree,
qualities included. Build them in C major and the extension's labels
read: **Imaj7, ii7, iii7, IVmaj7, V7, vi7, viiø7** (that slashed circle
is the half-diminished mark). The pattern is worth a minute of staring:
the two major-7 chords sit on I and IV, the minor 7ths pile up in the
middle, the [[half-diminished]] hides on the seventh degree, and exactly
one degree in the whole key produces a [[dominant seventh]]: the fifth.
One chord in the key gets the pulling flavor, and it lives a fifth above
home. That is not trivia. That is the plot of the next two articles.

Plain G already leans: its B is the [[leading tone]] from
[[minor-scales|Minor scales]], one semitone under home. Adding the F
loads a second magnet, because B and F together form a [[tritone]], the
restless interval that splits the octave exactly in half, and it
discharges by contrary motion: B rises a semitone to C while F falls a
semitone to E, both landing inside the C chord. One chord, two notes
under tension, one satisfying snap. Nearly every "resolving" gesture in
tonal music is this mechanism wearing different clothes.

Then there is the great exception. Blues, and everything downstream of
it, plays dominant seventh chords on *every* degree and resolves none of
them: the churn is kept as pure color, the lean repurposed as a way of
standing. A blues in C sits on C7 all day and calls it home. Theory says
restless; the music says comfortable; the music wins.

## In your music

The extension's chord recognizer names all four qualities on sight,
Cmaj7, C7, Cm7, Cm7b5, in clips and in the Harmonic Timeline, and the
Theory Reference panel lists your key's full seventh-chord shelf with
its jazz context. The cheapest experiments in this article: take any
triad loop you have and add the diatonic seventh to every chord, and
hear it turn into soul on the spot; strip the sevenths from a busy
progression and hear it go stadium-wide and anthemic, which is why rock
and EDM mostly live on triads; and drop exactly one V7 into the bar
before your loop returns home, then feel the room tilt toward the
downbeat. Why some orders of these chords feel inevitable while others
wander, the pull, mapped, is the next article. The one after names the
punctuation marks you have been playing all along.
