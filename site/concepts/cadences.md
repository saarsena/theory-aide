Four endings, four completely different kinds of silence afterward.
That is the whole subject. A cadence is not a chord, it is a *landing*,
and the marks differ in how much of the tension gets paid.

## The full stop and the amen

**V to I** is the [[authentic cadence]], and you already know its
machinery: this is the two-magnet snap from
[[seventh-chords|Seventh chords]] doing its official job. Leading tone
up, and with V7, the tritone discharging; everything owed gets paid, the
silence afterward is content. When a piece of tonal music is over and
knows it, this is almost always how it says so.

**IV to I** is the [[plagal cadence]], and its character comes from
what it lacks: IV contains no leading tone and no tritone, so nothing
snaps. The phrase settles rather than resolves, which is why churches
sing "amen" over it and why it works as a gentle tag *after* a real
ending, a hand on the shoulder once the argument is already won.

## The question mark and the swerve

The **[[half cadence]]** ends *on* V, and this is where the demo's
silent bar earns its keep: the phrase stops, the tension chord rings
into the gap, and nothing finishes. It is the question mark, and it is
also old news to you, because the question phrase in
[[phrasing|Phrasing]], the one that hung on D and refused to be filed
as done, was a half cadence wearing a melody. Verses do this in the
bar before the chorus for exactly the reason you would guess: a
section that ends on a question makes the next section the answer.

The **[[deceptive cadence]]**, V to vi, is the craftiest mark. V
promises home, and the ear's melodic demand is even honored, watch the
roll and see B rise to C exactly as the authentic version does, but
that C is now the third of A minor: the bass stepped aside and the
ground moved. A comma disguised as a period. Its classic job is buying
time: land on vi instead of I, and the phrase gets to run once more
before the real ending, the "one more time" technique, as Janet
Schmalfeldt named it, that composers have used to stretch final
phrases for centuries and producers reinvent every time a last chorus
needs to go around again.

## In your music

The extension knows the paid landings by name: run **Explain Harmony**
over these loops and the first two report an **authentic cadence** and
a **plagal cadence**; the half and deceptive endings carry no template,
the chords are labeled but the hanging is yours to hear, which is
fitting, since their whole point is what does not arrive. Three
experiments, one per mark: end your verse's last bar on V and notice
the chorus stop being optional; swap one final V-to-I for V-to-vi and
collect four free bars before the ending you were always going to
play; and after the last chorus has fully landed, tag IV to I once,
quietly, and hear why that move survived four centuries of church.
With this page, the harmony path's core run is complete, and the door
it unlocks is the climb's summit: which scales speak over which
chords, where everything this cluster built meets everything the modal
ascent taught.
