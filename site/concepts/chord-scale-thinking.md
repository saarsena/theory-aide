## The dice come back

The loop underneath is a ii-V-I in C, the falling-fifths chain from
[[progressions|Progressions]]: one bar of Dm7, one of G7, two of Cmaj7.
The dice write the melody, exactly as they did in the sandbox, and the
presets change only where the notes may come from. Play **Chord tones
only** first, then **One frozen scale**, then **Moving palette**, and
roll a few phrases of each:

Three ways of choosing, three different animals. All furniture and the
line is stiff. All floor and the line floats past the changes without
touching them. Furniture on the strong beats, floor between, and it
speaks. That difference is the entire craft this page names.

## Two readings of one object

Here is the part that sounds like a trick and is actually the point.
The "correct" scale assignments over this progression are D dorian for
Dm7, G mixolydian for G7, and C ionian for Cmaj7, and by the engine's
own accounting those three scales contain *identical notes*: the white
keys, three times over. Nothing about the pool changes at the bar
lines. What changes is the home, and you already know who decides
that: the bass, exactly as [[modes|Modes]] taught. A progression is a
bass that moves every bar, so the mode moves with it, under your
fingers, while the notes hold still. Chord-scale thinking is not a
list to memorize. It is the modes article, applied one bar at a time.
Read one direction, a chord is a scale with the walking notes removed;
read the other, a scale is a chord with the walking notes put back,
which is exactly how the [[seventh-chords|diatonic ladder]] was built:
stack a scale in thirds and the chords fall out.

## Land, walk, avoid

The working rule that falls out of all this fits in one sentence:
**land on [[chord tone|chord tones]], walk on scale tones, and mind
the avoid note.** Chord tones are furniture, always safe to sit on.
Scale tones are floor, made for moving across. And most chord-scales
hide one [[avoid note]], a scale tone that leans too hard on a chord
tone a semitone below it: over Cmaj7, that is F, rubbing against the
chord's E. Fine to walk through, rude to sit on. (Notice G7 has no
such note; dominants tolerate everything, one more reason the blues
lives there.) Seen from this summit, the pentatonic box explains
itself: five notes chosen so that every note is furniture and no note
is an avoid note. The box was not a beginner's toy. It was this rule,
pre-solved.

## The color dial, and your music

Once landing is handled, the [[chord-scale]] becomes a creative dial
rather than a rule. Press **Lydian color**: over the Cmaj7 bars, one
note turns, F to F#, and the same chord stands in different light, the
sharp 4th from [[modes|Modes]] now a soloist's decision instead of a
derivation. That dial goes much further, the melodic-minor scales
[[minor-scales|Minor scales]] hinted at wait over the spicier
dominants, but one turned note is the whole principle. In the DAW,
this page is a topline workflow: loop your progression, land your
hook's long notes on chord tones, walk between them with the parent
scale, and turn one color note when a section needs new light. The
extension's **Theory Reference** panel lists every diatonic chord with
its jazz context for exactly this use. And with that, the climb is
complete: it began with five notes you could not get wrong, and it
ends with a palette that moves, which was the assignment all along:
what speaks over what.
